# Action Economy
Players get 5 Initiative per turn to spend on movement, exploring map nodes, or performing city actions.

Some actions cost more Initiative. Some trainer abilities may influence initiative costs. Trainers who prefer nature may not spend an initiative to re-explore a space, but city actions cost an additional initiative, while trainers who prefer urban environments may have this interaction inverted.

# Map Traversal

## MOVEMENT

1 Initiative to move to an adjacent map node. Each time you enter a new map node you automatically "explore" it. More on that below.

## CITIES

There are 8 cities in the map, each inspired by a different American national park and representing a different type in our type chart which the City Arbiter uses as their main creature type for challengers. 

In a City players can rest at a stable. Each stable provides a Chanko Nabe (Hot Pot) to recover Creatures and provide unique buffs based on the city / stable. Using a stable completely recovers any team KO's, injuries or conditions.

Cities also have Carving Shrines where the player can spend resources to craft a totem to offer to wild creatures. Creatures need to be defeated in battle, then gifted a totem as an offering. If they accept and embrace the trainer, they can inhabit the totem and join the trainer on their journey. Totem carvings can be made generic to offer to any creature, or carved for specific types (or dual-types) to enhance the odds of alluring creatures of that type at the cost of being worse offerings for creatures of any other types. Totems carved to look like a specific creature are the best at drawing the attention of that one specific creature, but are in bad taste to offer to any others. As part of the offering process a totem may be paired with herbs, flowers, charcoal, chalk, incense, food, or any other item the trainer believes the creature may accept to be drawn to the totem gift. A lot of these are found in the wild through exploration.

At the start of the game players set their "home" city. This is where they will return in the case of a total team wipe. Resting at the stable here gives the trainer the benefits of an advanced Chanko Nabe.

Cities are also where you'll find Markets for buying various items. Energy boosts, health restores, condition cures, etc. Items are never used in battle.

Each city has an Arbiter. The Arbiter of a city uses creatures of a certain type. Defeating an Arbiter earns you an accolade (each arbiter may issue different types of prizes for beating them including stamps, badges, merits, tokens, etc.) Every 2 accolades increases your Trainer Tier. When you challenge an Arbiter, the difficulty of the challenge they present to you is set by your trainer tier. The higher your tier, the harder an Arbiter is expected to push you. At 0 Accolades you are Tier 1. At 8, you are Tier 5. Only Tier 5 trainers are invited to participate in the Creature Circuit at the end of turn 100.

## TOWNS

Like cities, towns are smaller and don't have Arbiters or Chanko Nabe, but they are safe from the events in the wilderness regardless. Usually a town will have some key item required to progress through the rest of the map or unlock a shortcut. Some of them have unique game mechanics like special markets for unique items, create trade posts, or so on.

# Exploration

Map nodes are defined by their biome. Mountains, Lakes, Forests, Roads, Swamps, etc. These inform some other elements of exploration like what Offering Materials can be found, or which wild Creatures will be encountered.

When you enter a new node you "explore" it. This is a dice roll with the following possible results:

1-2: Nothing.
3-4: Wild Creature encounter.
5-6: Discovered item.

## Nothing

When "nothing" is rolled the player may immediately move to a new node or explore their current area again by spending another initiative.

## Wild Creature

When a Wild Creature is encountered the following occurs:

1: A base "rarity" is calculated, set by the node the trainer is in. Some nodes have "rarer" creatures than others.
2: Rarity dice are rolled based on various factors like items, passives, trainer abilities, etc. The default is two dice rolled.
3: These dice have results showing a Star. Either a blank result (1-2), half star (3-4), or full star (5-6) are possible results.
4: Each full star adds to the rarity. Then the rarity and type are considered and a creature is generated from the appropriate pool of possibilities. Such as a "Common Water type" creature or an "Apex Astral type" creature based on the location and rarity rolls.

## Discovered Items

When an item is found another dice is rolled. Berries, Offering Materials, pre=carved Totems, and items which can normally be bought from the Market are all possible options. The Berry or Offering Material found is determined by the biome of the node being explored.

# Energy System

Creatures generate energy. There is an energy for each of the 8 types and a special "Prisma" energy which counts as any type.

Energy is required to learn attacks. Some attacks have a "Neutral" slot which can be occupied by any energy type. A "Fire Claw" for example might require 3 Fire and 1 Neutral energy to assign to a Creature. So long as the creature produces that much Energy, they can learn that move and use it in battle. 

Energy is not "spent" to lock-in a move. Rather it sets the capabilities of the creatures. It's more like a requirement than a resource.

# Creature Information Formatting

Creatures are displayed on a character sheet about the size of a large index card. On the front you'll find their name, type(s), rarity (Common, Prime, or Apex), Evolution Stage (1 - 3), their Wild Rating (indicating how hard they are to allure with offerings), their level, experience points, Nature (indicating their general attitude and some passive effects or abilities), Morale (indicating trust in their trainer), the creature art, their combat attributes (Vigor, Health, Stamina, Fatigue, Power, Armor, Focus, Ward, Speed, Ability, and Affinity), as well as their Energy Generation.

On the back of the character sheet is a flowchart of advancement nodes. When the creature earns 10 xp, they unlock a new node on the back of the chart. These might be access to special attacks, new energy, improved stats, or more. At the bottom of every chart is an Evolution to the next stage, except on stage 3 evolutions which instead have powerful capstone abilities which grow in strength based on the creature's morale.
