# The 8 Cities of Margos

## 1. Aurora Peak

Based on Denali National Park in Alaska, Aurora Peak is a cold climate in the northern part of our map and represents the Astral type. The aurora borealis phenomenon is a regular occurrence during the nights here and all sorts of Astral creatures call the surrounding area home, drawn to the clear night skies, high altitude, and purity of the air. The city itself is built of low structures half-sunk into the earth to take advantage of geothermal heat and natural insulation.

The Arbiter of Aurora Peak is **Salem**. Their arena is only open under the light of the aurora borealis and takes place on floating ice floes which run the risk of having unprepared creatures tip and sink into the frigid arctic water below.

**Cloud Island:** a portal in Aurora Peak opens at night (and all Winter) to Cloud Island, a ring of six cloud nodes (Gatecloud Landing, Nimbus Steps, Starfall Drift, Moonbow Arch, Thunderhead Rise, Stillwind Hollow) floating off the north-west coast. Salem only battles trainers who have walked a full lap of it. The island is cotton candy pinks and purples with a sinister dark purple running through it. Every step there costs +1 Initiative per Tier, and anything found there is only kept once the trainer escapes. In the middle lies the Eye of the Sky, home of the Legendary Nimbalune, open to trainers who have beaten Salem. In Winter, Aurora Peak is always under Aurora Night.

## 2. Hollow Crest

Based on Olympic National Park in Washington, Hollow Crest is a city nestled inside a temperate forest which gets seasonally cold and represents our Charm type. The moss covered woodland presents a picturesque fairy-forest type environment. The "city" itself is mostly made of cozy log cabins.

The Arbiter of Hollow Crest is **Fabian**. Their arena is made mostly of illusory terrain obfuscated by fog and glamorous lighting tricks. Fabian himself hides among the population of Hollow Crest in disguise, choosing to prank prospective challenging trainers to see their reaction before allowing them to challenge him to battle.

Navigation in the fog can be difficult in Hollow Crest. The road from Mistfall is open, but off its Fogbank Path a misty branch runs north-west, south of Aurora Peak, to the town of **Lantern Glade**. Only a Fog Lantern holding a **wisp** lights the way in. Wisps drift to a random wild node, town or city on the mainland; checking the lantern reveals where, and the trainer must walk there to catch it. The wisp slips away whenever the trainer leaves the Glade, so each visit needs a new one.

Behind Lantern Glade lie the **Rainbow Plains**, four meadows whose creatures all change to one random type each new day (odd-numbered turns). The ground takes on that type's colour: purple for Astral, pink for Charm and so on. Creatures there are always at least one rarity step higher, and finds are exotic, like those of the Forest Floor and Cloud Island.

## 3. Thunder Spike

Based on the Badlands National Park in South Dakota, Thunder Spike is a rolling and rugged stony desert environment. The static in the air raises the hair on the arms of passing travelers, and a massive storm is constantly rolling through the area somewhere. The electricity in the air attracts all types of Electric creatures and trainers looking to make offerings to them. Thunder Spike city is named so for the massive lightning rod which protects the city when the local storm moves through.

The Arbiter of Thunder Spike is **Watson**. His arena sits directly underneath the eponymous thunder spike and throughout battle will unleash charged volts upon the creatures fighting within. In play, the volts heal Electric creatures (dual types too) and shock everyone else.

## 4. Kilauea Forge

Based on the Hawai'i Volcanoes National Park in Hawaii. Kilauea Forge is a forge town on the volcanic southwest coast of the mainland, so Fire trainers can start their journey here like any other type. Offshore lies Kilauea island, the southern-most point of Margos, a tropical paradise where Fire type creatures enjoy the heat of the steam vents and lava tunnels. The island is reached by ferry (with a Ferry Pass) from the Forge's docks or from Mangrove Port, landing at Black Sand Cove.

## 5. Gloomshade

Based on the Redwood National Parks in California and representing our Grass type. Gloomshade is a city built into massive towering trees where the people live on elevated boardwalks and ringed platforms, high off the forest floor. The trees are so tall, and the canopy so thick, that sunlight doesn't manage to reach the forest floor. In most places in Gloomshade, looking over the edge of one of the boardwalks is like looking into a bottomless black pit. The creatures which occupy the forest floor are quite ferocious indeed, but for trainers brave enough to descend, they can find some of the most powerful Grass types in the world. For the less daring, there are plenty which occupy the areas surrounding the Gloomshade forest, or who live among the people up near the canopy.

The Arbiter of Gloomshade is **Dahlia**. Her arena is built somewhere in the Gloomshade forest floor, where only trainers brave and strong enough to dare to descend from the canopy can challenge her and earn her accolade, the Lily Badge. The Canopy Rope needed to descend is sold in a different town each game. Below Gloomshade lies the **Forest Floor**, a single dark node explored again and again: the trainer must follow signs of Dahlia's Trail (3, +1 per Tier) to find her arena, and loses the trail on leaving. Once Dahlia is beaten, it can still be explored for its creatures and exotic finds, with no trail to chase.

## 6. Houndmouth Canyon

Based on the Grand Canyon in Arizona, Houndmouth Canyon is an unfathomably massive canyon which acts as a sort of "endless rogue-like dungeon" for our game. It can be explored endlessly as the walls shift and move from all the Rock creatures living there making various tunnels.

The Arbiter of Houndmouth Canyon is **Cliff**. His arena is built out of a tall standing plateau and designed to have the edges carve off, making the standing space smaller and much more limited before a creature falls in and is defeated. In play, the plateau is a ring-out timer: it shrinks every round, and a challenger who hasn't beaten Cliff when it's gone loses.

## 7 Iron Coast

More loosely based on Acadia National Park in Maine than the others, Iron Coast is a series of pebble beaches with foggy Atlantic coast vibes. Colorful houses, lighthouses, a lot of seafaring paraphernalia around the city. The Steel type creatures here seem to share an origin with the mysterious ore which sweeps into the shore from some distant unknown ocean.

The Arbiter is **Stella**. Her arena is a floating fortress which docks only once a trainer has proven themselves worth of Stella's attention. Otherwise, it rests beneath the waves where she hones her skills. To prove themselves, trainers run the **Iron Coast Gauntlet**: 2 local trainers per Tier, beaten without leaving the city (see [Arbiters & Arenas](./11-arbiters-and-arenas.md)).

## 8. Mangrove Port

Based on the Everglades National Park in Florida. The city is built on stilts overlooking marshy waters and mangrove trees with roots which shift every few hours, making traversal here through the water nearly impossible for all but the native water creatures. Boardwalks and suspended bridges dominate the area.

The Arbiter is **Katy**. She has a mystical connection to the mangrove roots and can will them open or closed. She uses this to help her city, but also to deny unworthy trainers from ever finding her to issue their challenges. The Root Whistle that opens the roots is sold in a different town each game.

# Map Design

Legendary creatures wait at seven spots across the map, each at the end of a short branch path: Sunspire Summit, Ironwake Shoals and the Eye of the Sky (Cloud Island) in the north, and Willowheart Isle (central lakes), Dunecrown Mesa (southern desert), Boglight Hollow (south-eastern swamps) and Caldera Heart (Kilauea island) in the centre and south.

The map is a series of nodes between towns and cities. Every route between two towns or cities has 3–5 wild nodes, so the journey between them is a real trip with several chances to explore, find items and meet creatures. Each node represents a biome, each creature can be caught in a specific type of biome. Between Iron Coast and Gloomshade might be mostly coasts, roads, or forests for example.

Legendary Nodes are specific slots on the map where legendary creatures roam. These often require some other steps to be done first before they can be unlocked.

**Each Legendary exists once per game** (prototype rule). A Legendary waits at its node until it is beaten, whether it's recruited or driven off. While any trainer holds it, its node stays quiet. Releasing it at a Research Facility returns it to its node, where another trainer (or you again) can try for it. Legendaries also lose 1 Loyalty every turn and return to their node on their own at 0 Loyalty, so they cycle between trainers over a season.

## Towns with special services

**Quillhaven** sits on the meadows between Ridgeway and Saltmarsh Landing, home to the Margos **Research Facility**. Trainers can release a recruited creature into the researchers' care for coins: its level × 2 (Common), × 3 (Prime), × 4 (Apex) or × 5 (Legendary). Release is permanent, though the Codex keeps its record. You can't release your last creature, your only active one, or your starter, who shares a special bond with the trainer.

**Valor Ridge** sits in the hills between Mistfall and Thunder Spike, home to the Margos **Battle School**:
- **Enrolling:** pay coins and leave a creature. It earns 1 XP per coin, at 2 XP a turn, so a course takes half as many turns as the XP it buys. While it trains, it is away from your team.
- **Picking up:** collect it at the school any time. An early pick-up keeps the XP earned so far, and the rest is forfeited. You're notified at the end of the turn its course finishes. Its new level-up points are then spent on its chart as usual.
- **How it felt about the stay:** a d6 decides. 1–2 is rough (−2 Morale, −1 Loyalty), 3–4 is fine, and 5–6 is great (+2 Morale, +1 Loyalty).
- **Legendaries:** they keep losing Loyalty while away, and may have gone back to their grounds by the time you return.
- **Retraining:** the drill masters can also rework a creature: pick any new **Nature** (it keeps the number of perks it has unlocked, now from the new Nature), any overworld **Ability**, and/or any battle **Instinct**, from the full lists. Each change has its own price (a new Nature 💰 6, a new Ability 💰 8, a new Instinct 💰 10) and costs **1 Loyalty** when you pick the creature up, as the drills are harsh. The creature stays overnight: it's ready **the next morning** (the next day turn), and can't be picked up before then ("Come back in the morning to pick up …!"). You're told at the end of the night that it's ready. There's no mood roll. A Legendary's Loyalty keeps draining while it's away. (`world.ts` → `RETRAIN_COST`, `RETRAIN_LOYALTY`, `retrainBlock`, `enrollRetraining`, `nextMorning`, `pickUpTraining`.)
- The Trail Tutor and Drum Circle events (and the Creature Caretaker) change an Ability, Instinct or Nature for free, but only to one of three random options (see [Events](./10-events.md)).
