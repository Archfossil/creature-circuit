100-Turn Seasonal System: Four 25-turn seasons (Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter) governed by a 1–6 Weather Die that alters movement costs and type effectiveness.

Arbiter Progression: Defeating regional Arbiters grants Accolades/Badges required to enter the endgame.

Endgame Gauntlet (Turns 101–110): Round-robin matches against Circuit Captains every other turn, concluding with championship tie-breakers.

# Gameplay Timer

Each game lasts 100 rounds representing a full year. Every 25 rounds the seasons change. Turn 1 at the start of the game is in Spring. Turn 26 moves to Summer. Turn 51 starts Autumn, and turn 76 is the start of Winter.

At the start of a new round, a weather dice is rolled to determine the weather for that round. Weather dice are seasonally specific. Summer can have heat waves while Winter might have blizzards. The weather plays effects in combat and can change what types of creatures are encountered on the map nodes.

The goal of the game is to have a competitive team of creatures capable of winning difficult battles, to take them to the Creature Circuit and win a grand tournament. To be eligible, you must have collected 8 Merits from the Arbiters in the region and be present at the Creature Circuit on Turn 100. If you do not have all the merits, or you are not in the right place when Turn 100 ends, you miss your opportunity to compete, and your game is over.

## Endgame

If any players are eligible to compete in the Creature Circuit at turn 100, the game goes into the "Endgame" representing every round taken past 100.

In the Endgame, Circuit Champions and all qualifying players are divided into a bracket to face off against one another in a 3v3 elimination format. There must be a minimum of 8 participating trainers (backfilled with Champion NPCs if necessary.)

# Arbiters

Every Arbiter in the region has their own type specialty. Their arenas also invoke certain tricks to make challenging them more difficult based on the tier of the trainer. Each time an Arbiter is defeated, the winning trainer earns a Merit. Trainers will need 8 Merits to compete in the Endgame Creature Circuit.

A trainer with no merits is Tier 1. At two merits they become Tier 2. At 4, Tier 3. At 6, Tier 4. At 8 Merits the trainer is Tier 5 and eligible for competition in the Endgame.

When an Arbiter is challenged, they account for the challenging trainer's Tier. They use more powerful creatures and more tricky Arena effects against higher Tier trainers. This allows trainers to take on Arbiters in any order they wish as the difficulty will curve to respond to them.
